# Hokigotchi: five-minute pitch

Use the eight-slide deck. The timing includes the live demo. Presenter notes are also inside the PowerPoint. The words below are a rehearsal script, not a requirement to read every line.

## Slide 1 — Hokigotchi · 0:00–0:20

“Meet Hokigotchi, a financial practice game for student life. You take care of a little Hoki by building useful money habits. As you plan, track spending, and work toward a savings goal, your companion grows with you.”

## Slide 2 — A plan meets real life · 0:20–0:50

“Imagine a student bringing in $1,450 a month and spending $1,240. That leaves $210 toward their next goal. The plan looks manageable. Then their laptop breaks. What happens to the goal now? Which response can they afford? We want to make that tradeoff visible before they have to make the real decision.”

These are the demo's example amounts. Do not present them as average student finances.

## Slide 3 — Your habits grow Hoki · 0:50–1:30

“The daily game gives you a reason to return. Logging an expense earns XP. Setting a weekly budget earns XP. As you reach new milestones toward your own savings target, you get bigger bonuses. Those actions help Hoki hatch, grow, and unlock the world map.

“Rewards have limits, and they are not based on how much you spend. You can update a weekly plan without repeatedly collecting the same reward. The goal is a useful habit.”

If rehearsed, briefly switch to the main app, record one eligible expense, and show the progress notice. Skip this switch if it would interrupt the flow.

## Slide 4 — Chaos Mode · 1:30–3:00

Switch to `/chaos` or the offline backup. Start at the ready screen.

“Now let's practice the surprise. Here is our sample banking world, with a savings goal and an emergency reserve.”

Click **UNLEASH CHAOS**.

“Your laptop just broke. Would you buy new, go refurbished, or try a repair?”

Let a judge choose, then select their response.

“The choice changes the reserve, the goal date, and Hoki's response. Let's compare one other path.”

Select a second response. Point to the chart and goal date.

“The council explains the same calculation through safety, goals, everyday needs, and bills. The current explanations are rule-based. They make the consequence understandable without inventing a balance.”

## Slide 5 — The timeline · 3:00–3:30

Return to the deck.

“With the same starting plan, the original goal takes four months. Buying new pushes it to seven. Refurbished brings it to five, recovering two months compared with new. A successful repair keeps the original timeline. The model rebuilds the emergency reserve before adding money to the goal, so you can see the tradeoff.”

If asked, repair assumes a confirmed $180 fix. This is a scenario, not a claim that repair is always available or the best choice.

## Slide 6 — How it works · 3:30–4:00

“Next.js runs the app and our calculations. Capital One Nessie supplies simulated account balances for practice. Supabase gives us email-code accounts, private cloud saves, and an optional weekly league. The server calculates cloud rewards, and repeated requests cannot simply award the same action again. The league shows nicknames and habit scores while financial entries stay private.”

Only demonstrate a live Nessie refresh if it has already worked during rehearsal. Otherwise accurately identify the displayed setup as sample data.

## Slide 7 — The next chapter · 4:00–4:35

“We have the budgeting and companion loop, saved accounts, an opt-in league, and the interactive surprise scenario. Next we would connect more banking activity and make practice scenarios more personal. We also want to explore campus reward partnerships. Coupons and cashback are previews today; real rewards would need partners, funding, and verification.”

## Slide 8 — Close · 4:35–5:00

“Our next step is testing whether students return to the game, keep useful records, and understand these choices better. We want money habits to feel approachable enough to practice before a crisis. Hokigotchi: little Hokie, big future. Thank you.”

## If you are running late

Keep the judge-choice interaction. Shorten the technical explanation to one sentence and show the roadmap briefly. Finish on the final slide instead of adding another feature.
