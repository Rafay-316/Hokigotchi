# Judge Q&A

## What makes this different from a budget tracker?

Hokigotchi connects the tracking habit to a growing companion and lets players explore a surprise before making a decision. The visible example is the same laptop problem producing different reserve balances and goal timelines. We have not completed a market-wide novelty study or user-outcome trial.

## Is it only rewarding login?

No points are awarded merely for opening the app or signing in. An explicit daily check-in is one small quest. Expense logging, weekly planning, spending reviews, and new savings milestones also earn rewards.

## Can people farm points by entering fake expenses?

Daily caps, duplicate handling, one-time milestones, server-calculated cloud rewards, and request receipts limit repeated awards. Entries are still self-reported, so those controls do not verify a transaction happened. That is one reason real-money payouts are not enabled.

## Do wealthier students automatically win?

Expense rewards are per eligible entry, not per dollar. Savings rewards use progress toward the player's own goal. The shared league ranks habit points and active days rather than bank balances. This is a design choice; fairness still needs evaluation with users.

## What does Capital One Nessie contribute?

The existing backend reads simulated account balances from Nessie. The practice balance challenge and optional Chaos snapshot use those balances. The prototype does not yet import the full purchase history, pay bills, or connect each cloud user to an individual bank account.

## Is the council powered by AI?

The demo council is rule-based and explains deterministic calculations. A future model could help interpret goals or generate scenarios, but monetary outcomes should continue to come from explicit calculation rules. Do not describe this demo as a live multi-agent system.

## What happens when someone falls below their buffer?

The optional device-practice challenge can reduce weekly points, with caps. It preserves lifetime XP and owned cosmetics, and a later successful balance check can earn a recovery reward. Cloud balance challenges await individual account connections.

## Are the coupon and cashback offers real?

They are labeled previews. The project has no claimed partner agreements, funding, redemption process, or payouts. Real rewards would require all of those plus eligibility and fraud checks.

## How do you protect private information?

Cloud operations verify the player's session on the server. Database access is restricted, and opted-in rankings return nickname and habit scores rather than expenses or goals. This describes the implemented controls; it is not an independent security audit or a production-readiness claim.

## Why does repair not delay the goal in the example?

A successful $180 repair leaves $740 in reserve. The next $210 surplus restores $60 of reserve, leaving $150 for the goal. Three more monthly contributions finish the $700 target in month 4. Buying new leaves a much larger reserve gap and pushes the same goal to month 7.

## Why not use the checking balance immediately for the goal?

This scenario starts goal contributions at zero and treats monthly surplus as the amount available after normal expenses. Existing checking is not automatically assigned to the goal. It is a stated planning assumption, not a universal recommendation.

## What would you validate next?

Run a student pilot and measure useful logging, weekly return visits, and understanding of a scenario before and after play. Financial outcomes, retention improvements, and willingness to use the product remain hypotheses until measured.

## How could this sustain itself?

Potential directions include campus programs or funded partner rewards. Those are hypotheses rather than an established business model. The immediate goal is validating the habit and learning experience.
